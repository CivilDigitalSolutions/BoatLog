Rowing glossary: terms every new rower hears

Bow, stroke, crab, rating: the words you'll hear in your first week, explained in one line each.

By Civil Digital, the team building BoatLog · Last updated 23 October 2026

BoatLog is independent software from Civil Digital. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by British Rowing or any organisation named in this guide. Always check the current official guidance and follow your club's own procedures.

The terms

Bow
The front of the boat, and the rower sitting nearest to it.
Stroke
The rower nearest the stern who sets the rhythm; also one complete rowing movement.
Cox
The person who steers and directs the crew, usually in the stern or lying in the bow.
Sculling
Rowing with two oars (sculls) each. Sculling boats are marked with an x, such as 1x or 4x.
Sweep rowing
Rowing with one oar each. Sweep boats include the pair (2-), four (4- or 4+) and eight (8+).
Boat classes
1x single, 2x double, 2- pair, 4x quad, 4- coxless four, 4+ coxed four, 8+ eight. A plus means a cox; a minus means none.
Rigger
The metal or carbon frame that holds the oar out from the side of the boat.
Gate
The swivel on the rigger that the oar sits in, closed with a screw-down bar.
Blade
The flat end of the oar; also used for the whole oar.
Heel restraints
Ties that stop the heels lifting too far from the footplate, so feet come free in a capsize.
Bow ball
The rubber ball on the tip of the bow that reduces damage and injury in a collision.
Catch
The moment the blade enters the water.
Finish
The end of the stroke, when the blade comes out of the water.
Feathering
Turning the blade flat between strokes to reduce wind resistance.
Catching a crab
When a blade gets stuck in the water at the finish, jerking the handle into the rower.
Easy there
A call to stop rowing.
Hold it up
An emergency stop: blades squared in the water to stop the boat quickly.
Rating
Strokes per minute.
Split
Time per 500 metres, the usual pace measure on an ergometer.
Erg
An ergometer: an indoor rowing machine.
Head race
A time trial with crews starting one after another. See our head race guide.
Regatta
Side-by-side racing over a shorter course.
Launch
A motor boat used for coaching and safety cover.
Landing stage
The pontoon or bank where boats are launched and recovered.
Circulation pattern
The agreed traffic rules for a stretch of river, such as which side to keep to.
Stream
The speed of the river's current. Strong stream can stop rowing.
RowSafe
British Rowing's safety guidance for clubs.
Junior categories
Racing age groups such as J14 to J18, based on age on 1 September before the competition.
Masters
Veteran rowers, raced in categories by age.

Questions

FAQ

What does 4x mean in rowing?

A quad: four scullers, each with two oars. The x means sculling.

What is the difference between sculling and rowing?

Scullers have two oars each; sweep rowers have one. Both are called rowing.

What does catching a crab mean?

When a blade gets stuck in the water at the end of the stroke, pulling the handle into the rower.
