British Rowing club safety audit: a quick guide for clubs

The annual safety audit, in plain English, and how to have the evidence ready before the reminder lands.

By Civil Digital, the team building BoatLog · Last updated 23 October 2026

BoatLog is independent software from Civil Digital. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by British Rowing or any organisation named in this guide. Always check the current official guidance and follow your club's own procedures.

What the safety audit is

Each year British Rowing asks every affiliated club to complete a safety audit: a self-assessment of how the club manages safety, completed by the club and reviewed by your Regional Rowing Safety Adviser. It is built around RowSafe, British Rowing's safety code.

When it runs

British Rowing launches the audit annually in October for online submission. For the current cycle it went live on 6 October 2026, and it must be completed, submitted and accepted by your Regional Rowing Safety Adviser before 24 November 2026. Clubs that have not had an audit accepted by the deadline are suspended from racing from 10 December 2026 — so a missed audit is a racing problem as well as a safety one. Dates move each year: check British Rowing's club safety audit page and ClubHub for the current window, and tell your Regional Rowing Safety Adviser early if you have a legitimate reason you cannot make the deadline.

What you need ready

The audit is based on RowSafe and asks you to upload your safety documents to the club's Google Drive as part of the process. British Rowing lists these:

  • Safety policy.
  • Club safety plan.
  • Emergency response plan.
  • Risk assessments for club activities — and for training camps, if you run them.
  • Any other supporting evidence.

Beyond the documents, the questions are about what the club actually does:

  • A record of incidents and near misses, and what changed afterwards.
  • Evidence that boats and launches are checked and that defects are fixed.
  • Who holds which qualifications (swim tests, capsize drills, first aid, Powerboat Level 2), and when they expire.
  • How the club decides whether it is safe to go out, and how members know.

How BoatLog helps

Most audit answers are about records. BoatLog keeps them as the club works: incidents and near misses, repairs, the daily safety flag and member qualifications with expiry dates. When the audit opens, the evidence is already there.

Questions

FAQ

Who completes the British Rowing safety audit?

Usually the club's rowing safety adviser, working with the captain and committee. It is then reviewed by the Regional Rowing Safety Adviser.

Does BoatLog submit the audit for us?

No. The audit is completed through British Rowing. BoatLog keeps the club's safety records so the answers and evidence are easy to find.

What happens if we miss the deadline?

Clubs whose audit has not been submitted and accepted by the deadline are suspended from racing — for the current cycle, from 10 December 2026. If there is a legitimate reason you cannot meet it, tell your Regional Rowing Safety Adviser as early as possible. Check British Rowing's current guidance for this year's dates.
