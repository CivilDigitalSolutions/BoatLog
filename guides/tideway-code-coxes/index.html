The Tideway Code for coxes: a plain-English summary

If you row, steer or coach on the tidal Thames, the Tideway Code applies to you. Here is the short version.

By Civil Digital, the team building BoatLog · Last updated 22 October 2026

BoatLog is independent software from Civil Digital. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by British Rowing or any organisation named in this guide. Always check the current official guidance and follow your club's own procedures.

What the Tideway Code is

The Tideway Code is the code of practice for rowing and paddling on the tidal Thames, published by the Port of London Authority with the Thames Regional Rowing Council and Paddle UK. Its second edition was published in 2024. It applies to clubs based on the Tideway and to crews who visit to train or race, such as for the Tideway heads.

Key principles

  • The tidal Thames is a working river with large commercial and passenger vessels. Keep clear of them.
  • Follow the navigation rules and local circulation patterns set out in the Code.
  • The cox or steersperson is responsible for the boat's navigation.
  • Know the tide and stream before you go, and the warning systems on your stretch.
  • Be visible: lights and bright kit in poor light.

A club checklist

  • Every cox and sculler has read the current edition.
  • Visiting crews are briefed before they boat.
  • Your club's risk assessment refers to the Code.
  • Near misses on the Tideway are reported and shared.

Questions

FAQ

What is the Tideway Code?

The code of practice for rowing and paddling on the tidal Thames, published by the Port of London Authority with the Thames Regional Rowing Council and Paddle UK.

Does the Tideway Code apply to visiting crews?

Yes. It applies to anyone rowing on the tidal Thames, including crews visiting to race or train.

Where does the tidal Thames start?

The tidal Thames runs downstream from Teddington Lock. Above Teddington the river is non-tidal and managed by the Environment Agency.
