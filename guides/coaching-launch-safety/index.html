Coaching launch safety: kill cords, drivers and checks

The launch is the club's rescue boat. It needs a qualified driver, a kill cord and a quick check every time.

By Civil Digital, the team building BoatLog · Last updated 22 October 2026

BoatLog is independent software from Civil Digital. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by British Rowing or any organisation named in this guide. Always check the current official guidance and follow your club's own procedures.

The essentials

  • Kill cord: attached to the driver whenever the engine is running, with a spare on board.
  • Qualified driver: British Rowing guidance expects launch drivers to hold an RYA Powerboat Level 2 or equivalent. Check its current guidance and any rules for young drivers.
  • Pre-use check: fuel, kill cord, engine, hull, bung, and that the safety kit is on board.
  • Safety kit: throw line, buoyancy aids, first aid, a way to call for help, and a blanket in winter.

British Rowing has published safety alerts on launch driving and kill cords, and RowSafe covers launch safety.

Know who can drive

A coach who says "I've done my Powerboat 2" five years ago is common. Keeping a list of who holds the qualification, and when their first aid lapses, stops guesswork at the landing stage.

Questions

FAQ

Do you need a qualification to drive a rowing coaching launch?

British Rowing guidance expects launch drivers to hold RYA Powerboat Level 2 or equivalent. Check its current guidance and your club's rules.

Must a kill cord always be worn?

Yes. The kill cord should be attached to the driver whenever the engine is running.

What safety kit should a rowing launch carry?

A throw line, buoyancy aids, a first aid kit, a way to call for help and, in cold weather, a blanket or exposure bag.
