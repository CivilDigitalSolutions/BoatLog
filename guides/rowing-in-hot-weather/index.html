Rowing in hot weather: heat safety for clubs

Summer regattas and early mornings bring a risk clubs plan for less often than cold: heat.

By Civil Digital, the team building BoatLog · Last updated 22 October 2026

BoatLog is independent software from Civil Digital. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by British Rowing or any organisation named in this guide. Always check the current official guidance and follow your club's own procedures.

Know the signs

Heat exhaustion can show as headache, dizziness, nausea, heavy sweating, cramps and tiredness. Heatstroke is a medical emergency: confusion, a very high temperature, or someone who stops sweating. Call 999. British Rowing has published a safety alert on avoiding and treating heat exhaustion, and RowSafe covers heat.

Simple club rules

  • Move sessions earlier or later on very hot days.
  • Shorter pieces, more rest, and water in every boat.
  • Shade and water at the boathouse and at regattas.
  • Sun protection, hats and light kit.
  • Watch juniors, beginners and older rowers closely.

How BoatLog helps

The club's safety flag in BoatLog can carry a hot-weather rule for the day, and anyone can report a heat-related incident so the club can learn from it.

Questions

FAQ

Is it safe to row in hot weather?

With precautions. Clubs move sessions to cooler times, shorten pieces, and make sure everyone has water and shade.

What are the signs of heat exhaustion in rowers?

Headache, dizziness, nausea, heavy sweating, cramps and tiredness. Confusion or a very high temperature can mean heatstroke, which needs a 999 call.

Does British Rowing have hot weather guidance?

Yes. British Rowing has published a safety alert on heat exhaustion, and RowSafe covers heat.
