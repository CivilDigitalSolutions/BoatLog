What is a head race? A UK guide for rowers and clubs

The time trials of the rowing year: how they work, and what your club needs to sort out before the day.

By Civil Digital, the team building BoatLog · Last updated 22 October 2026

BoatLog is independent software from Civil Digital. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by British Rowing or any organisation named in this guide. Always check the current official guidance and follow your club's own procedures.

Head race vs regatta

A head race is a time trial. Crews start one after another, usually 10 to 20 seconds apart, and race a long course (often several kilometres) against the clock. The fastest time wins. A regatta is side-by-side racing over a shorter distance, usually in knockout rounds. Head season runs from autumn to spring; regatta season runs through the summer.

What happens on the day

  • Marshalling: crews boat at set times and wait in numbered order, sometimes for a long while in the cold.
  • Steering: the racing line matters, and overtaking has rules. Read the race's own instructions.
  • Penalties: time penalties for things like missing the marshalling order or steering offences.
  • Results: published by the organiser, usually on the day.

On the Tideway, the Tideway Code applies to everyone, including visiting crews.

The club's checklist

  • Entries made on time, and racing members with the right British Rowing membership.
  • Boats rigged, checked and loaded; see our trailer checklist.
  • Warm kit for marshalling, and a plan if the race is cancelled.
  • Damage reported as soon as crews are back.

Questions

FAQ

What is a head race in rowing?

A time trial in which crews start one after another and race a long course against the clock. The fastest time wins.

How is a head race different from a regatta?

Head races are timed processions over a long course, mostly in autumn to spring. Regattas are side-by-side races over a shorter course, mostly in summer.

Do I need British Rowing membership to race a head?

For affiliated competitions, racers generally need the right British Rowing membership. Check the race's entry rules.
