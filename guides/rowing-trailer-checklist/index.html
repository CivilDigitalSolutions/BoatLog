Rowing trailer loading and towing checklist

Taking boats to a head or regatta? Here is a checklist for the people loading and the person driving.

By Civil Digital, the team building BoatLog · Last updated 23 October 2026

BoatLog is independent software from Civil Digital. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by British Rowing or any organisation named in this guide. Always check the current official guidance and follow your club's own procedures.

The driver

  • Licence: if the driver passed their car test on or after 1 January 1997, a category B licence now lets them tow a trailer up to 3,500kg maximum authorised mass — no separate trailer test. GOV.UK calls this category BE, and DVLA adds it to the photocard at the next renewal, so a licence that does not show BE yet is not a problem. Drivers who passed before 1 January 1997 can usually drive a vehicle and trailer with a combined MAM up to 8,250kg. Check the driver's own licence record on GOV.UK rather than assuming.
  • Weight is the limit that bites: the trailer's MAM, the car's towing capacity, the nose weight and the combined train weight are four different numbers and the lowest one wins.
  • Vehicle: towing capacity, nose weight and mirrors suitable for the load.
  • Insurance: the vehicle and trailer are insured for towing.
  • Your club may have its own rules on who can tow.

Loading

  • Load heavy boats low and balance the trailer so the nose weight is right.
  • Pad and tie every boat at each support, with enough ties per boat.
  • Secure riggers, oars, slings and loose kit.
  • Mark any overhang clearly and check it is within the legal limits.

Before you set off

  • Lights board working, number plate fitted.
  • Tyres, wheel nuts and breakaway cable checked.
  • Hitch locked and jockey wheel up.
  • Walk round once more, and recheck ties after the first few miles.

Questions

FAQ

Do I need a special licence to tow a rowing trailer?

Usually not. A driver who passed their car test on or after 1 January 1997 can tow a trailer up to 3,500kg maximum authorised mass on a category B licence, with no separate trailer test. Drivers who passed before then can usually tow up to a combined 8,250kg. What matters next is weight — the car's towing capacity and nose weight — and your club's own rules on who may tow. Check the driver's licence record on GOV.UK.

How should boats be tied onto a trailer?

Padded at every support and tied down at each one with enough ties per boat, with riggers and loose kit secured separately.

Who checks the trailer before a trip?

The driver is responsible for the trailer being roadworthy. Many clubs also have a nominated trailer officer.
