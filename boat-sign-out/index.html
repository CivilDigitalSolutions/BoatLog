Boat sign-out and boathouse kiosk software for rowing clubs

A digital boat sign-out that replaces the paper book or whiteboard — and works even when the boathouse has no signal.

The problem with paper sign-out books and whiteboards

Every rowing club knows the sign-out book. It lives on a shelf by the boathouse door, or it's a whiteboard that gets wiped clean before anyone checks who actually signed the 4- back in. Handwriting is illegible, pages go missing, and nobody can say with certainty which boats are out, who's in them, or when they left — until someone starts asking questions after dark.

It matters more than it looks. A sign-out record is the first thing a safety adviser reaches for if an outing overruns, and it's the difference between "we think everyone's back" and actually knowing. It's also the thing that stops two crews turning up for the same boat at the same time.

How BoatLog's boat sign-out kiosk works

BoatLog replaces the book with a kiosk: a single shared tablet or phone mounted at the boathouse door that any member can use in a few taps. Sign a boat out, and it's logged — who, what boat, roughly when you expect to be back. Sign back in, and the record closes. The rest of the club can see, at a glance, which boats are out and which are free, so there's no double-booking and no guessing.

Because it's built around a shared device at the point where members actually stand — at the door, boat in hand — it fits the way a boathouse actually runs, rather than asking everyone to remember to open an app before they leave home.

Built for boathouses with poor signal

Most rowing club premises are sheds, containers or clubhouses by the water, and mobile signal at the water's edge is often patchy at best. A sign-out system that needs a live connection at the exact moment someone's rushing to get on the water isn't much better than the book it replaced.

BoatLog's kiosk is built offline-first: sign-ins and sign-outs are captured on the device itself, the moment they happen, and sync automatically once it picks up a connection again. Nobody needs to think about signal — they just sign the boat out and go.

Sign-out is the start of the safety record

A sign-out isn't just a log entry — it's the first data point in BoatLog's safety tools. The same record that shows a boat is out is what powers an overdue-outing alert if it doesn't come back when expected, and what a British Rowing incident report starts from if something goes wrong. If a boat comes back damaged, the crew can flag it on the same device — see damage reporting.

Questions

Boat sign-out & kiosk FAQ

What if the boathouse has no WiFi or mobile signal?

The kiosk is built to work offline. Sign-ins and sign-outs are captured on the device itself and sync automatically as soon as it picks up a signal again — you don't need a working connection at the point someone signs a boat in or out.

Can more than one member use the same kiosk device?

Yes. A kiosk is a single shared device — a tablet or phone mounted at the boathouse door — that any member can use to sign a boat out or back in. It's designed to replace a single paper book or whiteboard that the whole club uses.

Does BoatLog replace our whiteboard or sign-out book entirely?

That's the intent — a digital log that's always legible, always up to date, and never left at home or run out of pages. Most clubs keep a simple paper backup for the first few weeks while everyone gets used to the new routine, which is sensible.

Is boat sign-out linked to safety features like RAG flags and overdue-outing alerts?

Yes. Because every outing starts with a sign-out, that same record feeds BoatLog's safety tools — RAG flags for conditions and overdue-outing alerts when a boat hasn't been signed back in when expected.

Does the kiosk know which boats are already out?

Yes — the sign-out log shows which boats are currently out on the water and which are free, so members aren't relying on memory or a scribbled note to avoid double-booking a boat.
